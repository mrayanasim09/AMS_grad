\begin{abstract}
\textsc{Adam}'s convergence proof in the Online Convex Optimization (OCO) framework fails when informative gradients are sparse: the exponential moving average of squared gradients decays between bursts, violating $\Gamma_t \succeq 0$ and causing divergence to a suboptimal boundary. \textsc{AMSGrad} repairs this with a monotone running maximum. We present a from-scratch NumPy reproduction of the deterministic and stochastic counterexamples of Reddi et al., with live $\Gamma_t$ diagnostics and a six-optimizer comparison. Extending beyond replication, we derive a protected zero-crossing $s^*(C)$ and a traverse threshold $\alpha^*(C) \approx 2/\sqrt{C}$, validated against empirical bisection to within $3\text{--}6\%$, plus a sub-threshold pinned-band formula (errors $3\text{--}17\%$, consistent with weakening $+1$-anchoring). A 4,500-run stochastic sweep maps the $(\beta_2, C)$ failure surface with Wilson 95\% confidence intervals. Full cycle-averaged profiles reveal a non-monotone survival structure: broad survival at small burst scale, shrinking survival islands at intermediate burst scale, and empirical closure of all islands by $C \ge 75$ at fixed $\alpha = 0.80$, refuting our a priori power-law scaling hypothesis. A closing experiment confirms sharp \textsc{AMSGrad} convergence under $\alpha_t \propto 1/\sqrt{t}$ (mean $x_T = -0.988$), consistent with the $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{T})$ guarantee.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{Keywords:} Adaptive Gradient Methods, Adam, AMSGrad, Online Convex Optimization, Phase Boundary, Non-Convergence, Stochastic Diffusion.
