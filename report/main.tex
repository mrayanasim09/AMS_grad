\begin{abstract}
Adaptive gradient algorithms, led by \textsc{Adam}, are fundamental to deep learning optimization. However, \citet{reddi2018convergence} revealed a fundamental flaw in \textsc{Adam}'s convergence proof under the Online Convex Optimization framework: when informative gradients occur sparsely, exponential moving averages allow the second-moment estimate to decay, violating the positive semi-definiteness condition of the metric matrix ($\Gamma_t \not\succeq 0$) and causing divergence to suboptimal boundaries. To resolve this, \textsc{AMSGrad} enforces monotonic non-decrease of second-moment estimates via a running maximum ($\hat{v}_t = \max(\hat{v}_{t-1}, v_t)$). In this work, we present a complete, from-scratch NumPy reproduction and extension of the \textsc{Adam} $\to$ \textsc{AMSGrad} lineage. We faithfully reconstruct both deterministic (Theorem~3) and stochastic (Section~3) counterexamples, introducing real-time $\Gamma_t$ tracking that visualizes the theoretical breakdown. Furthermore, we execute a pre-registered extension evaluating the $(\beta_2, C)$ phase boundary across $4{,}500$ stochastic runs ($N=100$ seeds per cell). Our empirical results refute the simple $C^{-1}$ memory-horizon scaling law: under rigorous cycle-averaged metrics, the transition boundary ceases to exist beyond $C \ge 100$, where \textsc{Adam} fails unconditionally across all $\beta_2$. Mechanism discrimination probes demonstrate that large-$C$ divergence is $\beta_2$-independent and governed by post-burst first-moment drift. Finally, we discuss why \textsc{AMSGrad}'s historical max locking induces practical conservatism in non-stationary deep learning regimes.
\end{abstract}
