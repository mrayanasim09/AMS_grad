\section{Discussion \& Practical Implications}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Relation to Prior Theoretical Literature}
The stochastic and deterministic counterexamples are instances of the general constructions in \citet{reddi2018convergence} (Theorem~3, arXiv version 1904.09237). Their asymptotic result is that for any $\beta_2 < 1$, there exists a sufficiently large $C$ causing \textsc{Adam} to diverge. Our contribution is the finite-time $({\alpha}, \beta_2, C)$ phase diagram and the quantitative Regime II traverse threshold $\alpha^*(C) \approx 2/\sqrt{C}$, which predicts the lower opening boundary of the survival island to within 5\% at $C=100$ and $C=300$. The empirical disappearance of the survival island at $C \ge 75$ at fixed $\alpha=0.8$ is consistent with Reddi et al.'s asymptotic prediction but refines it: it is not that $\beta_2$ tuning simply fails at large $C$ --- rather, the whole survival island (which is narrow to begin with in Regime II) shrinks and closes as $C$ grows, for reasons the upper-edge theory does not yet fully explain.

\subsection{Practical Conservatism of AMSGrad}
\textsc{AMSGrad} successfully prevents v-collapse by enforcing $\hat{v}_t \ge \hat{v}_{t-1}$, restoring $\Gamma_t \succeq 0$ and $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{T})$ regret under $\alpha_t \propto 1/\sqrt{t}$. In practice, however, a single early outlier burst can lock $\hat{v}_t$ at a large value, permanently depressing coordinate learning rates --- a well-known conservatism tradeoff.

\paragraph{Reconciliation.}
On $\mathcal{F}=[-1,1]$ with $\mathbb{E}[g_t]=\delta$, instantaneous expected regret is bounded by $2\delta$, capping $\mathbb{E}[R_T] \le 2\delta T = \mathcal{O}(T)$ for all optimizers. The $\lambda > 1$ slopes for \textsc{Adam} and \textsc{AMSGrad} are initialization-offset artifacts: parameters start at $x_1=0$ close to $x^*=-1$, so early per-step regret is near zero. Fitting $R(t) \propto t^\lambda$ to an effectively affine trajectory with delay $t_0 > 0$ yields $\lambda = t/(t-t_0) > 1$. \textsc{AdaGrad}'s $\lambda = 0.84 < 1$ reflects its effective $\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{t})$ step decay even under nominally constant $\alpha$. The $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{T})$ guarantee for \textsc{AMSGrad} strictly requires $\alpha_t \propto 1/\sqrt{t}$, demonstrated in Figure~\ref{fig:stochastic_resolution}.
