\section{Discussion, Practical Implications \& Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Relation to Prior Theoretical Literature}
The boundary disappearance observed for $C \ge 75$ at $\alpha=0.80$ refines and contextualizes the asymptotic theorems of \citet{reddi2018convergence}. While Reddi et al.\ showed that for any fixed $\beta_2 < 1$ there exists a sufficiently large $C$ causing \textsc{Adam} to diverge, our parametric mapping provides the finite-time $(\alpha, \beta_2, C)$ phase diagram and establishes the quantitative scaling law $\alpha^*(C) \approx 2/\sqrt{C}$. This reveals that divergence at high burst scale is driven not solely by second-moment decay, but by first-moment post-burst drift overwhelming the finite survival window.

\subsection{Practical Conservatism of AMSGrad}
\textsc{AMSGrad} successfully resolves Regime I by enforcing $\hat{v}_t \ge \hat{v}_{t-1}$. This guarantees $\Gamma_t \succeq 0$ and restores sublinear regret $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{T})$ under decreasing step sizes. However, in non-stationary deep learning settings, the historical maximum $\hat{v}_t$ can be locked by a single early outlier gradient burst, depressing future coordinate-wise learning rates and inducing practical sluggishness.

\paragraph{Reconciliation: Apparent Super-Linear Slopes vs.\ $\mathcal{O}(T)$ Asymptotic Bounds.}
On any compact domain $\mathcal{F} = [-1, 1]$ with bounded subgradients ($\mathbb{E}[g_t] = \delta$), the instantaneous expected regret is $\mathbb{E}[g_t(x_t - x^*)] \le 2\delta$, strictly capping cumulative regret at $\mathbb{E}[R_T] \le 2\delta T = \mathcal{O}(T)$. The apparent slopes $\lambda > 1$ in Table~\ref{tab:lambda_slopes} are finite-window fitting artifacts:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Initialization Offset:} Because parameters initialize at $x_1 = 0$ near the optimal basin $x^* = -1$, early instantaneous regret is near zero. Fitting a pure power law $R(t) \propto t^\lambda$ to an affine trajectory $R(t) \approx c(t - t_0)$ with positive delay $t_0 > 0$ yields an apparent elasticity $\frac{d \ln R}{d \ln t} = \frac{t}{t - t_0} > 1$.
    \item \textbf{Constant Step Size vs.\ Theoretical Convergence:} Under fixed $\alpha = 0.8$, step sizes do not decay ($\alpha_t \not\to 0$). With effective drift $\mu \approx -28.3$ and noise scale $\sigma_{\text{eff}} \approx 18.7$ ($\hat{v}_t \approx 200$), \textsc{AMSGrad} maintains a persistent stationary distribution across $[-1, 1]$ with mean $\approx -0.08$, generating asymptotically linear regret $\Theta(T)$. The optimal sublinear bound $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{T})$ strictly requires $\alpha_t \propto 1/\sqrt{t}$, under which \textsc{AMSGrad} concentrates at $x^* = -1$ (Figure~\ref{fig:stochastic_resolution}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Data, Code, and Hardware Availability}
All experimental code, automated verification suites, raw CSV results, and plotting routines are publicly available. Experiments were executed on Apple M-series hardware running macOS 15, utilizing Python 3.14.6 and NumPy 2.2. Automated unit tests enforce exact $t=1$ bias correction ($\text{atol}=10^{-12}$), weak monotonicity of $\hat{v}_t$ ($\text{atol}=0.0$), projection bounds ($\mathcal{F} = [-1, 1]$), and 3-sigma statistical checks over $10^5$ draws.
