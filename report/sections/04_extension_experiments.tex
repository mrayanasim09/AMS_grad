\section{Extension: Phase-Boundary \& Memory-Horizon Scaling}
\label{sec:extension}

\subsection{Pre-Registered Hypothesis \& Scaling Framework}
While \citet{reddi2018convergence} proved that \textsc{Adam} diverges for specific pathological parameter choices, the global phase boundary separating convergence from divergence across hyperparameters remained uncharacterized. We pre-registered the following formal hypothesis:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Pre-Registered Scaling Hypothesis:} The transition threshold $(1 - \beta_2^*)$ separating \textsc{Adam}'s convergence from divergence scales inversely with the burst spacing $T_{\text{burst}} \approx \frac{C}{1+\delta}$, according to the power law:
\begin{equation}
    1 - \beta_2^* = k^* \cdot \frac{1 + \delta}{C} \iff \tau_2^* \propto T_{\text{burst}},
    \label{eq:scaling_hypothesis}
\end{equation}
yielding a theoretical log-log slope of $\frac{\partial \log(1 - \beta_2^*)}{\partial \log C} \approx -1.00$.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{Pre-Registered Decision Rule.} If the fitted log-log OLS slope on deterministic transition points satisfies $\text{slope} \in [-1.2, -0.8]$, the memory-horizon hypothesis is confirmed. Deviations outside this interval falsify simple linear scaling and indicate higher-order dynamical moment interactions.

\subsection{Experimental Grid Sweep Design}
We evaluated a two-dimensional parameter grid:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Burst scale: $C \in \{10, 30, 100, 300, 1000\}$;
    \item Dimensionless parameter: $k = (1 - \beta_2) C \in \{0.10, 0.25, 0.50, 1.00, 1.50, 2.00, 3.00, 5.00, 10.00\}$, floored at $\beta_2 \ge 0.05$;
    \item Sample size: $N = 100$ independent seeds per grid cell ($4{,}500$ stochastic runs total) with $\delta = 0.10, \alpha = 0.8, T = 50(C+1)$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Empirical Results \& Hypothesis Evaluation}

\paragraph{Deterministic Boundary OLS Fit.} Using log-space bisection at $T = 200(C+1)$, we localized the deterministic transition boundary $\beta_2^*(C)$ where $x_T$ flips from survival to divergence:
\begin{itemize}
    \item For $C = 10$: $k^* \approx 2.33 \implies 1 - \beta_2^* \approx 0.233$ ($\beta_2^* \approx 0.767$).
    \item For $C = 30$: $k^* \approx 0.76 \implies 1 - \beta_2^* \approx 0.0255$ ($\beta_2^* \approx 0.9745$).
    \item For $C \ge 100$: Adam fails across all tested $\beta_2 \le 0.99999$ ($1 - \beta_2^* < 10^{-4}$).
\end{itemize}

An Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) regression of $\log(1 - \beta_2^*)$ on $\log C$ yields:
\begin{equation}
    \text{Slope} = -3.78 \pm 1.60 \quad (95\%\text{ CI: } [-5.37, -2.18], \ R^2 = 0.88).
\end{equation}
As illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:loglog_boundary}, the empirical slope is substantially steeper than the predicted $-1.00$.

\paragraph{Decision Rule Verdict: \textsc{Refuted (Steeper Vulnerability)}.} The pre-registered decision rule formally refutes the hypothesis of simple linear scaling. The physical mechanism underlying this steeper slope is straightforward: in an objective of burst length $C$, the non-burst duration lasts for $C-1$ consecutive steps. For large $C \ge 100$, the first moment $m_t$ saturates to the negative gradient direction ($m_t \approx -1$) within $\tau_1 = \frac{1}{1-\beta_1} = 10$ steps, leaving over $90$ steps where \textsc{Adam} takes full-magnitude steps towards the wrong boundary $+1$. Thus, \textsc{Adam}'s vulnerability compounds super-linearly with burst sparsity.

\paragraph{Stochastic Data Collapse \& Wilson Intervals.} Figure~\ref{fig:data_collapse} plots the empirical failure fraction against the dimensionless memory ratio $\rho = \frac{\tau_2}{T_{\text{burst}}} = \frac{1+\delta}{(1-\beta_2)C}$, equipped with exact 95\% Wilson score confidence intervals. The data exhibits a structured transition curve with low residual MSE ($0.0347$).

\paragraph{Saturation Verification.} Re-running transition cells at doubled horizon $T_{\text{sat}} = 2 \times T = 100(C+1)$ confirmed that failure fractions remain statistically stationary (Table~\ref{tab:saturation_check}), verifying that our results capture asymptotic dynamical equilibria rather than finite-time transients.
