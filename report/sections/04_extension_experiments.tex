\section{Extension: Phase Boundary \& Mechanism Discrimination}
\label{sec:extension}

\subsection{A Priori Scaling Hypothesis}
\begin{quote}
\textbf{A Priori Hypothesis:} The boundary $(1-\beta_2^*)$ scales as $C^{-1}$, i.e., the log-log slope $\partial \log(1-\beta_2^*)/\partial \log C \approx -1.00$.
\end{quote}
\paragraph{Decision rule.} A fitted slope in $[-1.2, -0.8]$ supports the hypothesis. The empirical $k^*(C)$ sequence from bisection (Table~\ref{tab:det_cycle_boundary}; source: \nolinkurl{results/deterministic_boundary_cycle_metric.csv}) is wildly non-monotone (values $k^* = 2.26, 0.30, 1.50, 2.51, 2.25, 0.52$ for $C=10,30,40,50,60,70$), with swings of $\sim 8\times$ and no single-valued power law. This \emph{non-monotonicity is itself the refutation}: the a priori hypothesis is rejected.

\subsection{Measurement Validity \& Stochastic Failure Surface}
Single-point $x_T$ is phase-dependent in periodic deterministic objectives. We therefore use the cycle-averaged metric:
\begin{equation}
    \bar{x} = \frac{1}{C}\sum_{t=T-C+1}^T x_t, \quad \text{Dwell} = \frac{1}{C}\sum_{t=T-C+1}^T \mathbb{I}(x_t > 0.5).
    \label{eq:cycle_metric}
\end{equation}
The stochastic sweep (Section~\ref{sec:reproduction}) uses single-point $x_T$, which is appropriate there: stochastic arrivals are aperiodic, so $x_T$ is not systematically phase-biased.

Figure~\ref{fig:stochastic_heatmap} shows the full failure surface (source: \nolinkurl{results/phase4_grid_results.csv}). For $C=10$, failure rates are moderate across all $k$ (24\%--57\%), reflecting the small burst size. For $C=1000$, failure rates are uniformly high (exact row mean $84.0\%$ across cells, non-monotone in $k$: row reads 73, 91, 84, 82, 88, 86, 89, 84, 79\%) --- attributed to severe v-collapse at high $k$, with the small-$k$ cells additionally experiencing Regime II drift. The stochastic results are not directly comparable to the deterministic bisection: at $C=10$, $\beta_2=0.99$ (stochastic: $\sim 33\%$ failure) the aperiodic stochastic arrivals allow diffusion into either basin, even when the deterministic construction would always survive. This is consistent with the broad stationary distribution predicted by the Fokker-Planck analysis.

Figure~\ref{fig:xbar_profiles} displays the full $\bar{x}(k)$ profiles at $\alpha=0.8$ (Table~\ref{tab:det_cycle_boundary} summarises the survival structure):

\paragraph{C = 10 (broad survival).}
The traverse threshold is $\alpha^*(10) \approx 1.85 > 0.8$: the Regime II analysis says $\beta_2 \to 1$ should \emph{not} produce survival at $\alpha=0.8$. Yet Fig.~\ref{fig:xbar_profiles} shows survival across $k \in [0.05, 9.99]$ ($\min_k\bar{x}(k) = -0.948$), extending continuously to $k \to 0$ (the $\beta_2=0.999$ control has $k=0.01$ with $\bar{x}=-0.9486$). This small-burst regime is not covered by Regime II theory: $v_t$ does not fully decay between bursts even at moderate $\beta_2$, and the $C=10$ burst is too small to drive strong ratchet dynamics. Continuous bisection finds a local sign-change edge ($k^*=2.26$) within a non-monotone profile, not a single survival boundary. At low $\beta_2$ (high $k$), \citet{reddi2018convergence}'s stability condition $\beta_1 < \sqrt{\beta_2}$ is violated ($\sqrt{0.05} \approx 0.22 < \beta_1=0.9$), meaning the divergence mechanism is not guaranteed, contributing to survival.

\paragraph{C = 30, 40, 50, 60, 70 (empirical survival islands; only $k \to 0$ is Regime II).}
At intermediate $C$, $\alpha^*(C)$ falls below $0.8$, so survival opens at high $\beta_2$. The profiles confirm islands that shrink with increasing $C$. At $C=30$ and $C=70$, the 80-point grid ($\Delta k = C/80$) samples 1 cell at $k=0.05$ before crossing the zero line, while continuous bisection pinpoints the exact boundaries at $k^*=0.30$ and $k^*=0.52$.

\paragraph{C $\ge$ 75 (island closes).}
Profiles at $C=75$ and $C=100$ show $\min_k\bar{x}(k) > 0$ for all $k$ ($+0.029$ at $C=75$, $+0.172$ at $C=100$): the survival island has closed empirically.

\subsection{Derivations: Regime II Theory (Validity: $k \ll 1$)}
When $\beta_2=0.9$ ($\tau_2=10$, Regime I), $v_t$ decays rapidly; across a grid of $\alpha \in [0.05, 1.0]$ in steps of $0.05$ ($T_{\text{cycles}}=800$), \textsc{Adam} fails unconditionally with $\bar{x} \ge +0.80$ (source: \nolinkurl{results/mechanism_probes/deterministic_alpha_sweep_P1.csv}). Conversely, in Regime II ($\tau_2 \gg T_{\text{burst}}$, i.e., $k=(1-\beta_2)C \ll 1$), $v_t$ remains flat at $\tilde{v}_t \approx \mathbb{E}[g^2] \approx C$.

\paragraph{Derivation of Zero-Crossing $s^*$.}
After a burst $g_1=+C$, the debiased first moment for non-burst steps is $\tilde{m}_t \approx \beta_1^t(1-\beta_1)C - (1-\beta_1^t)$. Setting $\tilde{m}_{s^*}=0$ yields:
\begin{equation}
    s^*(C) = \frac{\ln\left((1-\beta_1)C+1\right)}{\ln(1/\beta_1)}.
    \label{eq:s_star_exact}
\end{equation}
For $C=100$: $s^*=22.76$ steps.

\paragraph{Derivation of Traverse Threshold $\alpha^*(C)$.}
For steps $s^*+1$ to $C$, \textsc{Adam} advances rightward at rate $\approx \alpha/\sqrt{C}$ per step. Full traversal ($\Delta x_{\rm right} \ge 2$) requires:
\begin{equation}
    \alpha^*(C) = \frac{2\sqrt{C}}{C - s^*(C)} \xrightarrow{C\gg s^*} \frac{2}{\sqrt{C}}.
    \label{eq:alpha_star_derived}
\end{equation}

As shown in Table~\ref{tab:alpha_star_validation}, predicted $\alpha^*$ values match empirical bisection to within $3.0\%$ at $C=100$ and $5.8\%$ at $C=300$.

\paragraph{Derivation of Sub-Threshold Pinned Band.}
When $\alpha < \alpha^*(C)$, the rightward sweep cannot cross the domain and the trajectory pins at $+1$. The cycle mean is:
\begin{equation}
    \bar{x} \approx 1 - \frac{\alpha(C-s^*(C))}{2\sqrt{C}}.
    \label{eq:pinned_formula}
\end{equation}
At $C=100$: predicted $0.620$ at $\alpha=0.10$ (observed $0.602$, error 3.0\%) and predicted $0.240$ at $\alpha=0.20$ (observed $0.205$, error 17.1\%). The systematic overestimate at larger $\alpha$ is expected: as $\alpha$ grows, the orbit extends leftward and the $+1$-anchoring assumption weakens.

\paragraph{Regime II Validity Condition.}
The derivation assumes $v_t \approx \text{const}$, which requires $k=(1-\beta_2)C \ll 1$. At $C=300$, the observed survival window spans $[\alpha^*=0.1375,\, \alpha_{\rm up}\approx 0.155]$ (width ratio $\approx 1.13$), substantially narrower than the $\sim1.82$ ratio at $C=100$. At $C=1000$, $k = (1-\beta_2)C = 1.0$ even at $\beta_2=0.999$, so the validity condition is never satisfied: no Regime II window exists. The upper edge $\alpha_{\rm up}$ of the survival window lacks a closed-form derivation and is an empirical, $C$-dependent quantity.

\subsection{Small-$C$ Stochastic Arrival Timing Diagnostic}
At $C=10$ ($T=550, \delta=0.10, \alpha=0.8, \beta_1=0.9, \beta_2=0.5, N=500$ seeds; see Table~\ref{tab:frozen_hyperparams}, source: \nolinkurl{results/mechanism_probes/terminal_burst_timing_diagnostic.csv}), stochastic outcomes are governed by the arrival timing of late bursts before horizon $T$. Diagnostic evaluation reveals a strong point-biserial correlation between failure ($x_T > 0.5$) and time elapsed since the last burst ($r_{\rm pb}=0.312$, $p=9.8\times10^{-13}$). Failed seeds experienced $11.76 \pm 0.41$ steps since their last burst vs.\ $6.00 \pm 0.27$ for surviving seeds.
