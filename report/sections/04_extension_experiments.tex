\section{Extension: Phase Boundary \& Mechanism Discrimination}
\label{sec:extension}

\subsection{A Priori Scaling Hypothesis}
\begin{quote}
\textbf{A Priori Hypothesis:} The boundary $(1-\beta_2^*)$ scales as $C^{-1}$, i.e., the log-log slope $\partial \log(1-\beta_2^*)/\partial \log C \approx -1.00$.
\end{quote}
\paragraph{Decision rule.} A fitted slope in $[-1.2, -0.8]$ supports the hypothesis. The empirical $k^*(C)$ sequence from bisection (Table~\ref{tab:det_cycle_boundary}; source: \nolinkurl{results/deterministic_boundary_cycle_metric.csv}) is wildly non-monotone (values $k^* = 2.26, 0.30, 1.50, 2.51, 2.25, 0.52$ for $C=10,30,40,50,60,70$), with swings of $\sim 8\times$ and no single-valued power law. This \emph{non-monotonicity is itself the refutation}: the a priori hypothesis is rejected.

\subsection{Measurement Validity \& Stochastic Failure Surface}
Single-point $x_T$ is phase-dependent in periodic deterministic objectives. We therefore use the cycle-averaged metric:
\begin{equation}
    \bar{x} = \frac{1}{C}\sum_{t=T-C+1}^T x_t, \quad \text{Dwell} = \frac{1}{C}\sum_{t=T-C+1}^T \mathbb{I}(x_t > 0.5).
    \label{eq:cycle_metric}
\end{equation}
The stochastic sweep (Section~\ref{sec:reproduction}) uses single-point $x_T$, which is appropriate there: stochastic arrivals are aperiodic, so $x_T$ is not systematically phase-biased.

Figure~\ref{fig:stochastic_heatmap} shows the full failure surface (source: \nolinkurl{results/phase4_grid_results.csv}). For $C=10$, failure rates are moderate across all $k$ (24\%--57\%), reflecting the small burst size. For $C=1000$, failure rates are uniformly high (mean $\approx 85\%$ across cells, non-monotone in $k$: row reads 73, 91, 84, 82, 88, 86, 89, 84, 79\%) --- attributed to severe v-collapse at all $k$, with the small-$k$ cells additionally experiencing Regime II drift. The stochastic results are not directly comparable to the deterministic bisection: at $C=10$, $\beta_2=0.99$ (stochastic: $\sim 33\%$ failure) the aperiodic stochastic arrivals allow diffusion into either basin, even when the deterministic construction would always survive. This is consistent with the broad stationary distribution predicted by the Fokker-Planck analysis.

\subsection{Survival Set Profiles \& Deterministic Boundary Mapping}

Figure~\ref{fig:xbar_profiles} displays the full $\bar{x}(k)$ profiles at $\alpha=0.8$ (Table~\ref{tab:det_cycle_boundary} summarises the survival structure):

\paragraph{C = 10 (wide broad survival).}
The traverse threshold is $\alpha^*(10) = 2\sqrt{10}/(10 - s^*(10)) \approx 2.06 > 0.8$: the Regime II analysis says $\beta_2 \to 1$ should \emph{not} produce survival at $\alpha=0.8$. Yet Fig.~\ref{fig:xbar_profiles} shows survival across almost all $k \in [0.05, \sim10]$ ($x_{\min}(\bar{x}) = -0.948$). This is a regime our analytic framework does not cover: at small burst scales, \textsc{Adam}'s $v_t$ does not fully decay between bursts even at moderate $\beta_2$, and the $C=10$ burst is too small to drive strong ratchet dynamics. The bisector at $C=10$ found a local sign-change edge ($k^*=2.26$) within a non-monotone profile, not a single survival boundary. The low-$\beta_2$ behavior at $C=10$ may also be related to \citet{reddi2018convergence}'s stability condition $\beta_1 < \sqrt{\beta_2}$: at $\beta_2=0.05$, $\sqrt{\beta_2} \approx 0.22 < \beta_1=0.9$, meaning the \emph{divergence mechanism} is not guaranteed by the theorem; this likely contributes to the survival island at low $k$. This regime is left as an open question.

\paragraph{C = 30, 40, 50, 60, 70 (narrow Regime II islands).}
At intermediate-to-large $C$, $\alpha^*(C)$ falls below $0.8$, so survival \emph{should} exist in a window $[\alpha^*, \alpha_{\text{up}}]$ at $\beta_2 \to 1$. The profiles confirm narrow islands at $k \ll 1$ (high $\beta_2$) that shrink with increasing $C$: at $C=30$, $n_{\text{cells}}=1$ ($k\approx0.05$); at $C=50$, $n_{\text{cells}}=4$ ($k\in[0.05,1.94]$); at $C=70$, $n_{\text{cells}}=1$ ($k\approx0.05$). The non-monotone $k^*(C)$ sequence (Table~\ref{tab:det_cycle_boundary}) reflects the bisector finding different edges of these islands, not a monotone boundary.

\paragraph{C $\ge$ 75 (island closes).}
The minimum cycle mean $\min_k\bar{x}(k)$ rises above $0$ at $C=75$ ($\bar{x}_{\min}=+0.029$): the survival island has vanished. This disappearance is an \emph{empirical finding}; the theory below predicts the lower opening threshold $\alpha^*(C)$ but does not yield a closed-form upper edge.

\subsection{Derivations: Regime II Theory (Validity: $k \ll 1$)}
The following analysis applies in Regime II ($\tau_2 \gg T_{\text{burst}}$, i.e., $k=(1-\beta_2)C \ll 1$), where $v_t$ remains approximately flat at $\tilde{v}_t \approx \mathbb{E}[g^2] \approx C$.

\paragraph{Derivation of Zero-Crossing $s^*$.}
After a burst $g_1=+C$, the debiased first moment for non-burst steps is $\tilde{m}_t \approx \beta_1^t(1-\beta_1)C - (1-\beta_1^t)$. Setting $\tilde{m}_{s^*}=0$:
\begin{equation}
    s^*_{\rm exact}(C) = \frac{\ln\bigl((1-\beta_1)C+1\bigr)}{\ln(1/\beta_1)}.
    \label{eq:s_star_exact}
\end{equation}
For $C=100$: $s^*_{\rm exact}=22.76$ steps. A first-order Taylor approximation $\ln(1/\beta_1)\approx 1-\beta_1 = 1/\tau_1$ gives:
\begin{equation}
    s^*(C) \approx \tau_1\ln\bigl((1-\beta_1)C+1\bigr),
    \label{eq:s_star_approx}
\end{equation}
yielding $s^*(100)\approx 23.98$ (5.4\% overestimate relative to exact; this error propagates into $\alpha^*$).

\paragraph{Derivation of Traverse Threshold $\alpha^*(C)$.}
For steps $s^*+1$ to $C$, the trajectory advances rightward at rate $\approx \alpha/\sqrt{C}$ per step. Full traversal ($\Delta x_{\rm right} \ge 2$) requires:
\begin{equation}
    \alpha^*(C) \approx \frac{2\sqrt{C}}{C - s^*(C)} \xrightarrow{C\gg s^*} \frac{2}{\sqrt{C}}.
    \label{eq:alpha_star_derived}
\end{equation}
Empirical validation (bisection at $\beta_2=0.999$, $T_{\rm cycles}=800$): observed $\alpha^*=0.2514$ vs.\ predicted $0.2631$ at $C=100$ (4.8\% error relative to observed bisection); observed $\alpha^*=0.1375$ vs.\ predicted $0.1304$ at $C=300$ (5.1\% error). All reported errors are relative to the empirical bisection value.

\paragraph{Derivation of Sub-Threshold Pinned Band.}
When $\alpha < \alpha^*(C)$, the rightward sweep cannot cross the domain and the trajectory pins at $+1$. The cycle mean is:
\begin{equation}
    \bar{x} \approx 1 - \frac{\alpha(C-s^*(C))}{2\sqrt{C}}.
    \label{eq:pinned_formula}
\end{equation}
At $C=100$: predicted $0.620$ at $\alpha=0.10$ (observed $0.602$, error 3.0\%) and predicted $0.240$ at $\alpha=0.20$ (observed $0.205$, error 17.1\%). The systematic overestimate at larger $\alpha$ is expected: as $\alpha$ grows, the orbit extends leftward and the $+1$-anchoring assumption weakens.

\paragraph{Regime II Validity Condition.}
The derivation assumes $v_t \approx \text{const}$, which requires $k=(1-\beta_2)C \ll 1$. At $C=300$, the observed survival window spans $[\alpha^*=0.1375,\, \alpha_{\rm up}\approx 0.155]$ (width ratio $\approx 1.13$), substantially narrower than the $\sim1.82$ ratio at $C=100$. This narrowing is consistent with the $k\ll 1$ approximation becoming less accurate as the window shrinks and $v_t$ fluctuations grow. At $C=1000$, $k = (1-\beta_2)C = 1.0$ even at $\beta_2=0.999$, so the validity condition is never satisfied: no Regime II window exists. The bisection boundaries at $C \in \{40, 50, 60\}$ (Table~\ref{tab:det_cycle_boundary}) lie at $k \in [0.5, 2.5]$ --- borderline for $k\ll 1$ --- which is why they are labeled as empirical islands rather than theory-predicted windows. The upper edge $\alpha_{\rm up}$ of the survival window lacks a closed-form derivation and should be treated as an empirical, $C$-dependent quantity.

\subsection{Small-$C$ Stochastic Arrival Timing Diagnostic}
At $C=10$ ($T=550, \delta=0.10, \alpha=0.8, \beta_1=0.9, \beta_2=0.5, N=500$ seeds; see Table~\ref{tab:frozen_hyperparams}, source: \nolinkurl{results/mechanism_probes/terminal_burst_timing_diagnostic.csv}), stochastic outcomes are governed by the arrival timing of late bursts before horizon $T$. Diagnostic evaluation reveals a strong point-biserial correlation between failure ($x_T > 0.5$) and time elapsed since the last burst ($r_{\rm pb}=0.312$, $p=9.8\times10^{-13}$). Failed seeds experienced $11.76 \pm 0.41$ steps since their last burst vs.\ $6.00 \pm 0.27$ for surviving seeds.
