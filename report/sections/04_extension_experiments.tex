\section{Extension: Phase Boundary \& Mechanism Discrimination}
\label{sec:extension}

\subsection{Pre-Registered Hypothesis \& Scaling Framework}
While \citet{reddi2018convergence} established that \textsc{Adam} can diverge on synthetic counterexamples for specific hyperparameters, the global boundary separating convergence from divergence across $(\beta_2, C)$ space remained uncharacterized. We pre-registered the following formal hypothesis:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Pre-Registered Scaling Hypothesis:} The divergence threshold $(1 - \beta_2^*)$ scales inversely with the gradient burst spacing $T_{\text{burst}} \approx \frac{C}{1+\delta}$, according to the power law:
\begin{equation}
    1 - \beta_2^* = k^* \cdot \frac{1 + \delta}{C} \iff \tau_2^* \propto T_{\text{burst}},
    \label{eq:scaling_hypothesis}
\end{equation}
yielding a theoretical log-log scaling slope of $\frac{\partial \log(1 - \beta_2^*)}{\partial \log C} \approx -1.00$.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{Pre-Registered Decision Rule.} If the fitted log-log OLS slope on deterministic transition points satisfies $\text{slope} \in [-1.2, -0.8]$, the memory-horizon hypothesis is confirmed. Deviations outside this band refute simple linear scaling.

\subsection{Measurement Validity: Phase Invariance}
In periodic or sparse stochastic counterexamples, parameter position oscillates sharply within each cycle of length $C$. As demonstrated in Section~\ref{sec:reproduction}, $x_t$ plunges to $-1.0$ at the burst step ($g_t = +C$) and rapidly ascends to $+1.0$ during the $C-1$ negative steps ($g_t = -1$). Consequently, a single-point terminal metric $x_T$ is phase-dependent (spread $\Delta x = 2.0$ across a cycle at $C=100$). To ensure measurement validity, we evaluate time-averaged metrics over the final cycle:
\begin{equation}
    \bar{x} = \frac{1}{C} \sum_{t=T-C+1}^T x_t, \qquad \text{Dwell Fraction} = \frac{1}{C} \sum_{t=T-C+1}^T \mathbb{I}(x_t > 0.5).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Decision Rule Verdict: \textsc{Refuted (Boundary Disappearance)}.}
The pre-registered memory-horizon hypothesis is formally refuted. Rather than maintaining a smooth $C^{-1}$ power-law transition, \textbf{the boundary ceases to exist for $C \ge 100$}: \textsc{Adam} fails unconditionally across all $\beta_2 \in [0.05, 1 - 10^{-6}]$. Fitting a power-law slope across clipped probe floors is mathematically invalid; the true physical finding is that \textsc{Adam}'s failure transitions from conditionally preventable (at small $C$) to completely inescapable (at large $C$).

\subsection{Mechanism Discrimination: Post-Burst Drift}
To explain why large $C$ induces unconditional divergence, we executed controlled mechanism discrimination probes (Figure~\ref{fig:p3_trace} and Table~\ref{tab:p1_p2_probes}):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{$\beta_2$-Independence (Probe P1):} At $C=100$, sweeping $\beta_2$ from $0.99$ to $1 - 10^{-6}$ ($\tau_2 = 10^2 \to 10^6$) yields an invariant cycle mean $\bar{x} = +0.173$ and dwell fraction $0.50$. The second-moment memory horizon is completely detached from the divergence threshold at large $C$.
    \item \textbf{First-Moment Drift Condition (Probe P2):} Over $C-1$ negative steps, the first moment saturates to $\tilde{m}_t \approx -1$ within $\mathcal{O}(\tau_1)$ steps ($\tau_1 = \frac{1}{1-\beta_1} = 10$). Over the remaining $C - \mathcal{O}(\tau_1)$ steps, \textsc{Adam} accumulates positive displacement $\Delta x_{\text{cycle}} \approx \alpha \frac{C - \mathcal{O}(\tau_1)}{\sqrt{v_{\text{burst}}}} \approx \frac{\alpha C}{C \sqrt{1-\beta_2}} = \frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{1-\beta_2}}$. Divergence occurs whenever this post-burst drift overcomes the single-step burst retraction, defining a traverse threshold $\alpha \ge \alpha_{\text{crit}}$ that is uniform in $\beta_2$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Collapse Quality \& Windowed Regret Exponent}
Evaluating data collapse against the memory ratio $\rho = \frac{\tau_2}{T_{\text{burst}}}$ reveals that universal collapse holds only conditionally: for $C \in \{10, 30, 100\}$, the interpolated $0.5$-crossing $\rho_{50}$ spans $0.51$ decades ($[0.127, 0.408]$), while for $C \ge 300$, failure exceeds $0.5$ across all probed $k$.

Finally, we report the windowed log-log regret exponent $\lambda = \frac{\Delta \log R_t}{\Delta \log t}$ over $t \in [0.1T, T]$ on the stochastic benchmark ($C=20, \delta=0.05$): \textsc{Adam} exhibits $\lambda = 1.2275$ (linear-to-superlinear regret under boundary pinning), while \textsc{AMSGrad} ($\lambda = 1.3069$) and \textsc{AdaGrad} ($\lambda = 1.4197$) reflect expected sublinear regret scaling bounded by finite-time domain constraints.
